Law Firm Website RFP Template

Copy these questions into an email or a document and send the same list to every agency you are considering. By Legal Web Agency.

Comparing website proposals is hard when every agency describes its work differently. A short request for proposal (RFP) forces like-for-like answers. Below is the template we suggest firms send, including to us. Each section links to a guide that explains what a good answer looks like.

Part 1: About our firm (you fill this in)

  • Practice areas, ranked by the fees they bring in.
  • Cities and counties we serve, and languages our clients speak.
  • Our current website address, and what we think is wrong with it.
  • How new clients reach us today (calls, forms, referrals, ads).
  • Our budget range and the date we need the site live.

Part 2: Questions for the agency

Experience and process

  1. Which law firm sites have you built in the last two years, and can we speak to one of those firms?
  2. Who will actually do the design, writing and development? Is any of it outsourced?
  3. What are the steps from kickoff to launch, and what do you need from us at each step?

Background: how to choose a web designer for your law firm.

Ownership

  1. Will the domain, hosting, website files and analytics accounts be registered in our firm's name?
  2. If we stop working together, what do we keep, and how is the site handed over?
  3. Which platform will you build on, and why? Is it a custom design or a template?

Background: law firm website hosting, custom law firm website design and law firm website builders.

Compliance

  1. How do you check page content against our state's attorney advertising rules?
  2. Which disclaimers will the site carry, and where?
  3. How do you handle accessibility (WCAG), and do you use overlay widgets?

Background: attorney advertising rules by state and is your law firm website ADA compliant?

Content

  1. Who writes the pages? Will each practice area get its own page?
  2. Will you review our existing content before rewriting it?

Background: law firm website content writing and law firm website content audit.

Search and AI visibility

  1. If we are redesigning, how will you protect our current rankings (redirect map, URL plan)?
  2. What structured data (schema) will you add for the firm and attorneys?
  3. How will the site be prepared for Google AI Overviews and AI assistants?
  4. Is ongoing SEO part of this proposal or separate? What does it cost per month?

Background: redesigning without losing rankings, schema markup for law firms, Google AI Overviews for law firms and law firm SEO cost per month.

Speed, leads and tracking

  1. What mobile loading targets do you commit to?
  2. How will calls and form submissions be tracked and reported to us?
  3. What will the contact and intake forms ask?

Background: Core Web Vitals on 14 law firm sites, conversion tracking for law firms and the law firm intake form template.

After launch and price

  1. What does monthly maintenance include (updates, backups, security, form tests)?
  2. Please give a fixed price for the build, a separate monthly price for maintenance and SEO, and list anything not included.

Background: law firm website maintenance and law firm website pricing.

Part 3: How to compare the answers

Red flags: the agency keeps the domain or hosting in its own name; no plan for redirects; "guaranteed #1 rankings"; vague monthly fees; no answer on advertising rules.

Good signs: specific law firm examples, a written page plan, clear ownership, and separate, itemized prices.
